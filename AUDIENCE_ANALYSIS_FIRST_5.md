## Shared audience pattern across the first 5 products

The products are not only educational PDFs. They are positioned as practical lead magnets with 100% resale rights, which creates several different buyer motives.

### Main buyer motives

1. Personal use
   - The buyer wants to learn the topic and apply it in their own work or life.

2. Lead magnet use
   - The buyer wants a ready PDF asset to collect emails, phone numbers or subscribers.

3. Resale use
   - The buyer wants to resell the digital product and keep 100% of payments.

4. Business/agency use
   - The buyer wants ready frameworks for client work, internal marketing, consulting or team training.

5. Content-product infrastructure use
   - The buyer wants to expand their own product line without producing a full product from scratch.

### Common objections

- Is the PDF actually practical or just generic theory?
- Can I legally resell it?
- Is it suitable for my niche?
- Can I use it as a lead magnet without editing?
- Is the price justified for a PDF?
- Will it help with real business outcomes, not just learning?
- What exactly is inside?
- Is the GOODLY bonus included in my purchase case?

### Common trust-building needs

- Clear list of what is inside.
- Format, volume and price shown clearly.
- Explanation of 100% resale rights in simple terms.
- Use cases separated: personal use, lead magnet, resale, team/client use.
- Honest note that the GOODLY “NEW” bonus is only for new users and not included when the product is received as a lead magnet.

## Product 1 — Вирусные видео: формулы создания и продвижения

### Primary audience segments

1. SMM specialists and content marketers
   - Need repeatable short-video formats, hooks and retention mechanics.
   - Buy motive: use in content production and client work.

2. Online entrepreneurs and experts
   - Need reach, subscribers and sales through video without relying only on paid ads.
   - Buy motive: personal/business application.

3. Owners of online schools, clubs and courses
   - Need video content to warm up audiences and move viewers toward applications or purchases.
   - Buy motive: funnel/content system improvement.

### Secondary audience segments

- Freelancers and consultants who sell content or marketing services.
- Sales specialists who need video content for trust and inbound leads.
- Marketing department heads who need a shared framework for a team.

### Awareness levels

- Problem-aware: “My videos do not get views or retention.”
- Solution-aware: “I need formulas, hooks and better short-video structure.”
- Product-aware: “This PDF gives a 182-page system and resale rights.”

### Key pains

- Low video reach.
- Low retention.
- No system for repeatable formats.
- Fear that algorithms suppress content.
- Difficulty turning views into leads or sales.

### Desired outcomes

- Understand why people share videos.
- Create stronger openings and hooks.
- Build content formats, not one-off clips.
- Repackage content across platforms.
- Connect viral content with product/offer logic.

### CTA angle

“Open the Goodly product page and check the full PDF guide on viral video formulas, promotion and resale rights.”

### Analytics signals to watch

- Clicks from sections about hooks, retention and reach.
- Scroll depth on guide contents.
- CTA clicks after resale-rights explanation vs after content-benefit explanation.

## Early strategic conclusions

1. The first 5 products should not be treated as isolated “course reviews”. They form a practical library for entrepreneurs, experts and marketers.
2. The strongest shared segments are online entrepreneurs, experts/consultants, SMM/content marketers and online school/course owners.
3. Each page needs at least two CTA narratives:
   - practical use of the PDF topic;
   - resale/lead-magnet use with 100% resale rights.
4. Analytics must distinguish whether users click because of the topic value or because of resale/lead-magnet value.
5. Product pages should be written only after analytics events and page template rules are defined.
