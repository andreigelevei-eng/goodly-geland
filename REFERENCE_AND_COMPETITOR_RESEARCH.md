# Reference and competitor research — first 5 Goodly products

Date: 2026-06-01
Repository: `andreigelevei-eng/goodly-geland`
Public site: `https://goodly.obzornik.online`

## Purpose

This file adds the missing research layer before public product page writing:

1. reference analysis — what strong review, affiliate, lead magnet and digital product pages do structurally;
2. competitor/SERP analysis — what users already see in search results for each topic cluster;
3. opportunity mapping — how Goodly review pages can be more useful without inventing product facts.

No public product pages should be written until this layer is completed for the selected topic cluster.

## Rule: facts vs market context

Separate two kinds of information:

### Product facts

Allowed source: only the provided Goodly product page text or verified product page data.

Examples:

- PDF volume;
- price;
- guide contents;
- resale rights;
- bonus conditions;
- stated audience;
- stated use cases.

### Market/context facts

Allowed source: competitor pages, SERP results, platform docs, public research, public articles.

Examples:

- common search intents;
- competing page structures;
- standard objections;
- common CTA patterns;
- search-result formats;
- gaps in competitor content.

Never mix market context into product promises. If competitors talk about an outcome but the Goodly product page does not, we can use it only as audience context, not as a product claim.

## Research order for each product/topic

1. Define topic cluster.
2. Define target audience and intent.
3. Search references and competitors.
4. Extract structural patterns.
5. Identify gaps and risks.
6. Draft page skeleton.
7. Fill product facts only from verified Goodly source text.
8. Add UTM CTA logic.
9. Only then write public copy.

## Reference types to collect

### 1. Product review / affiliate review pages

What to inspect:

- title pattern;
- who the product is for;
- pros/cons;
- what is inside;
- price/format block;
- CTA placement;
- comparison or alternatives;
- trust disclaimers;
- FAQ;
- schema/structured data.

What to borrow structurally:

- clear summary near top;
- “who it is for / not for” section;
- neutral review tone;
- CTA after useful information, not only at the top.

What to avoid:

- fake personal experience;
- exaggerated claims;
- unsupported guarantees;
- hidden affiliate intent.

### 2. Digital product / PDF landing pages

What to inspect:

- promise structure;
- table of contents presentation;
- bonus block;
- rights/licensing explanation;
- price anchoring;
- urgency/timer mechanics;
- purchase flow.

What to borrow structurally:

- concise format and volume block;
- transparent rights explanation;
- content list grouped by user problem.

What to avoid:

- copying seller page word-for-word;
- overusing urgency;
- repeating every sales phrase without review filtering.

### 3. Lead magnet pages

What to inspect:

- value proposition;
- audience promise;
- exchange mechanics;
- what user receives;
- where it fits into funnel;
- privacy/trust wording.

What to borrow structurally:

- explain practical use as lead magnet;
- separate “use personally” from “use in marketing”.

What to avoid:

- implying the user gets Goodly’s customer database or traffic;
- unclear bonus/rights conditions.

### 4. Resale-rights / PLR / MRR product pages

What to inspect:

- how rights are explained;
- what is allowed/prohibited;
- how buyers are reassured;
- how use cases are separated.

What to borrow structurally:

- plain-language “what resale rights mean” block;
- warnings about checking conditions;
- separation of resale, lead magnet and personal use.

What to avoid:

- promising easy income;
- implying guaranteed profit;
- suggesting illegal or unclear reuse.

## Competitor/SERP query map

### Cluster 1 — Viral videos / short-form content

Seed queries:

- `как создать вирусное видео`
- `формулы вирусных видео`
- `крючки для коротких видео`
- `как удерживать внимание в reels shorts tiktok`
- `продвижение коротких видео без рекламы`

What to analyze:

- whether SERP is educational articles, agency blogs, YouTube videos or tool pages;
- common structure of advice;
- whether competitors connect views to sales/leads;
- content gaps around systematic formats and repackaging.

### Cluster 2 — Personal growth without burnout

Seed queries:

- `личный рост без выгорания`
- `как развиваться без перегруза`
- `саморазвитие без стресса`
- `микропривычки для развития`
- `как ставить цели без давления`

What to analyze:

- whether pages are psychology blogs, coaching pages, productivity articles or course offers;
- how they explain burnout risk;
- whether they give frameworks or only generic advice;
- gaps around sustainable systems for entrepreneurs/experts.

### Cluster 3 — Faster learning / memory apps / speed reading

Seed queries:

- `приложения для скорочтения`
- `приложения для запоминания информации`
- `интервальные повторения приложения`
- `визуальное мышление интеллект карты обучение`
- `как учиться быстрее и запоминать больше`

What to analyze:

- app list articles vs method articles;
- whether users look for tools, techniques or complete system;
- trust signals around apps and learning science;
- gaps around combining speed reading, memory and visual thinking.

### Cluster 4 — Emotional resilience for leaders

Seed queries:

- `эмоциональная устойчивость лидера`
- `как руководителю справляться со стрессом`
- `управление эмоциями руководителя`
- `лидерство под давлением`
- `эмоциональный интеллект в переговорах и продажах`

What to analyze:

- management blogs vs psychology articles vs corporate training pages;
- whether content is practical or abstract;
- how competitors handle stress, conflict and decision-making;
- gaps around daily routines and business-pressure context.

### Cluster 5 — Writing cheat sheets / copywriting formulas

Seed queries:

- `формулы продающих текстов`
- `шаблоны продающих постов`
- `хуки для постов`
- `формулы заголовков AIDA 4U`
- `как писать экспертно но простым языком`

What to analyze:

- formula/listicle pages vs copywriting courses;
- how examples are presented;
- whether pages cover posts, emails, stories and landing blocks together;
- gaps around ready-to-use cheat sheets and fast writing.

## First-pass public research notes

These notes are preliminary. They should guide deeper manual review, not replace it.

### SEO and affiliate/review risk

Search engines have been tightening against low-quality affiliate or “parasite SEO” pages that are irrelevant, thin or exploit host reputation. This reinforces the need for standalone, useful, topic-relevant Goodly pages with clear value and no copied filler.

### Learning tools and memory context

For faster learning pages, the market includes well-known concepts such as spaced repetition and flashcard systems. The Leitner system is a widely used spaced-repetition method using flashcards with increasing review intervals. This supports making the page structure explain “tools + method + use case”, without claiming the Goodly PDF teaches anything not present in the product text.

### Emotional resilience context

Public definitions of emotional resilience usually emphasize maintaining adequate behavior and functional stability under stress, plus emotion management without losing rational thinking. This fits the audience-analysis layer for leaders, but product claims must still come only from the Goodly page text.

### Speed reading context

Speed-reading-adjacent queries may include attention and visual perception tools like Schulte tables. This suggests competitors may blend speed reading, attention training and focus apps; our page should separate what is inside the Goodly PDF from broader educational context.

### Copywriting context

Copywriting competitors often use formulas, templates and repeatable message structures. For Goodly writing-cheat-sheet pages, the opportunity is to organize formulas by use case: post, email, landing block, headline, first line and CTA.

## Page skeleton implications after reference/SERP analysis

A safer product review page should likely include:

1. Short neutral summary.
2. Verified facts table: format, pages, price, store URL, rights, bonus.
3. Who this product may fit.
4. What problem the topic solves.
5. What is inside, grouped from the verified product contents.
6. How the PDF can be used: personal use, lead magnet, resale, team/client work.
7. What to check before buying.
8. CTA with mandatory UTM.
9. FAQ based only on verified facts and common objections.

## What not to do

- Do not write “best product” claims without comparison research.
- Do not claim personal experience using the PDF.
- Do not invent modules, results, author credentials or guarantees.
- Do not copy competitor wording.
- Do not publish clean outbound Goodly links.
- Do not create cross-project references.
